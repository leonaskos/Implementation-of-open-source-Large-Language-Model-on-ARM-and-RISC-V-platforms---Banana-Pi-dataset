To calculate the derivative of the function \( f(x) = 3x^2 + 5x - 7 \), we apply the power rule for differentiation. The power rule states that if you have a function of the form \( ax^n \), its derivative is \( n \cdot a \cdot x^{n-1} \).

Let's apply this rule to each term in the function:

1. For the term \( 3x^2 \):
   - The derivative is \( 2 \cdot 3 \cdot x^{2-1} = 6x \).

2. For the term \( 5x \):
   - The derivative is \( 1 \cdot 5 \cdot x^{1-1} = 5 \).

3. The derivative of the constant term \(-7\) is \(0\), since the derivative of any constant is zero.

Combining all these results, we get the derivative of \( f(x) = 3x^2 + 5x - 7 \) as:

\[ \frac{d}{dx} (3x^2 + 5x - 7) = 6x + 5 \]

So, the derivative of the function is \( 6x + 5 \).
